Item 3 is blank in the outline and the ask was for 4–x, so item 3 reads as

## Trajectory A — "The Midway Cafe"

The institution hands over its own argument.

**4) Boston, a back room, a guy with a slide of a bird.** (2:30)
Chris talking about indigo birds in Cameroon at the Midway Cafe, which is where
this whole thing started. The useful detail is not the birds. It is that the
format was a person who cared, a beer, and no reason for anyone to be there.
*Decide and say which:* first-hand or second-hand. The talk can afford either and
cannot afford the audience wondering.

**5) The same room, twenty-odd years later, eight thousand miles away.** (3:00)
Three talks off the Fort Collins stage. This is the laugh.

**6) One example from outside a bar.** (2:00)
Either of his own two, past tense, as evidence that the effect is not a bar
effect. Short. Two is evidence; five is a CV.

**7) The turn, and the close.** (0:45)

**Where it goes wrong:** two origin stories competing — Nerd Nite's and his — and
a room that already knows what Nerd Nite is getting told its own history. If
item 4 runs long it becomes a history lesson delivered to the people who live
there.

**Strongest if:** he wants the argument to come from the institution rather than
from him, and the Midway Cafe story is first-hand.

## Trajectory B — "Nobody arrives caring about worms"  ← recommended

The laughs come first and the argument falls out of them. No sentence anywhere
has to claim that any of this matters.

**4) Three talks, as things that happened in a room.** (4:00)
The longest beat, and it is the catalogue. The pack's three are well chosen —
pipe organs, worms, Berlioz: one absurdly ambitious, one unglamorous, one funny.
**What this beat needs is one sensory detail each — what the speaker *did*, not
what the talk was about.** "He brought a pipe" beats "he explained pipe organ
construction." The Berlioz title is read aloud and then nothing is added to it.

**5) The thing hiding in the list.** (1:45)
Not one of those is a topic anyone walked in caring about. Nobody goes to a bar
wanting to know about worms. They leave caring about worms. Said once, flat, as
an observation about the list he just read — not as a thesis, which item 3
already did.

**6) The same content, flattened.** (1:15)
A recording of the worm talk is worms. The medium is what gets named here, never
the machine — the machine is the one thing that can never be there and it is not
in this talk.

**7) His own two, briefly.** (1:15)
Past tense, fourth in line on purpose, evidence rather than biography.

**8) The turn, and the close.** (0:30)

**Where it goes wrong:** five items in 8:45 is the tightest of the three, and
item 6 is one careless sentence away from arguing for the point instead of
showing it. If the laughs in item 4 run hot — and they should — item 6 is the one
to drop whole. Cutting it costs the talk nothing; item 5 already carries the
load.

**Strongest if:** the room is loose and the three sensory details are good. This
is the trajectory that puts the joy in the texture and keeps the argument
invisible.

## Trajectory C — "You cannot plot a course without one"

The abstract already promised this one, nearly word for word: *a map between us,
an understanding of the world we are in. You cannot plot a course without one.*
This trajectory is the only one that collects on that line.

**4) The map.** (1:30)
Pick the promise back up from the abstract. A room like this draws a map between
the people in it. That is the claim; the next item is the mechanism.

**5) What you do when there is no map.** (2:30)
This is where the KC-130 belongs — mid-talk, as mechanism, never as a
credential, and never in the opening. Dead reckoning: known heading, known
speed, known time, and a correction every single time you get a real fix. You
fly a computed position and you do not trust it longer than you have to.
Then the hinge, in one line: **every talk you sit through is a fix.**

**6) The fixes.** (3:00)
Three talks off his stage. Same material as Trajectory B item 4, doing different
work — each one is a position report from somebody else's trajectory, handed over
by a person who was actually there.

**7) Trajectories converge.** (1:15)
Item 1's thought comes back and closes. Everyone in the room has been running
their own solution the whole time. Tonight is a common fix.

**8) The turn, and the close.** (0:30)

**Where it goes wrong:** the moment the metaphor gets explained it dies. It has
to be load-bearing and unmentioned — "every talk is a fix" said once, then never
referenced again. Also the only trajectory with the military material in a
structural position, which is the position the operating file says it must not
occupy as a credential. Mechanism is fine there; introduction is not.

**Strongest if:** he wants the talk to use his own vocabulary rather than Nerd
Nite's, and he is willing to let one metaphor carry three items without ever
naming it. **This is also the trajectory the Luke Delaney material fits**, if it
goes in at all — item 5, replacing the KC-130 rather than joining it. That call
is his and only his.

## Slides, given Tuesday

All three trajectories want the same slide deck, because the delivery is
extemporaneous and bullets would commit him to a script he does not want.

- **One slide per item, plus the talk titles.** Ten to twelve slides, not thirty.
- **One line of text, maximum, per slide.** A talk title set large is a slide. A
  photograph is a slide. A list of four sub-points is a teleprompter and it will
  flatten the delivery.
- **The Berlioz title gets a slide to itself** and he says nothing over it.
- Beamer, `\usetheme{default}`, no navigation bars, no frame numbers, no section
  headers. Dark ground, one typeface. The deck should be invisible.
- **No slide for the close.** The turn happens with nothing on the screen, or
  with whatever was already up. A slide that says "You are." does the work for
  him and robs it.

## Constraints, restated because they are easy to lose at midnight

- Joy is the register, never the subject. Nothing argues for it. Nothing concedes
  to despair first.
- The machine stays out.
- No instruction to the room. Not once.
- Military past tense, never the opening position.
- The close is five words and nothing follows it:
  **You had to be there. You are.**
