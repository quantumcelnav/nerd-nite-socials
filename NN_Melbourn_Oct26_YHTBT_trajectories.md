## THE THESIS — his, 11 Oct 2026. Trajectory B is chosen and rebuilt around it.

In his words: **"for us people it is where the people are that the cognition
happens."** We have not sent people to Mars. We have sent probes, rovers and
instruments. A chronicle of those instruments' findings is incomplete without
the hypothesis of the people who built the probe and the understanding of the
people interpreting the data.

He also named the recursion himself: a talk called "You Had to be There", about
talks the Melbourne audience was not at, which were often about events that
*those* audiences were not at either.

**The recursion is not a problem to hide. It is the proof, and it is the laugh.**
A retelling fails when it carries only the data. It survives when a person who
was there carries the frame — the hypothesis going in, the interpretation coming
out. "You had to be there" is what people say when they transmitted the events
and left the cognition behind.

His own covering note to Melbourne already said the same thing without knowing
it: *"Chris talking about indigo birds at the Midway Cafe turned that bar into
Cameroon for twenty minutes, and that is the only reliable magic trick I know."*
Chris was not in Cameroon that night either. The room got Cameroon anyway.

And it collects on the abstract: a map is a model, not a data stream. *You cannot
plot a course without one.*

> **Venue, corrected 13 Oct.** The Fort Collins venue is the **Wolverine Farm
> Publick House**, not "a bar". His correction, and it is not pedantry — the
> talk's entire argument is that specific places and specific people are what
> survive retelling, so getting the venue wrong in the room would undercut it.
> The **Midway Cafe in Boston** genuinely was a bar and stays described that way.

## THE REFACTOR — his, 13 Oct. The thesis word is now ENERGY.

He sharpened it: *"you had to be there" is what someone says when their
description does not convey the **energy** of actually being there* — and for
something long ago or far away, it also means **you had to be at the talk about
the thing.**

**That turns the recursion into a ladder**, and the ladder is the argument:

| rung | |
|---|---|
| **living it** | the Donner Party, 1846. Worms in the soil. The organ being built. |
| **retelling it** | Hannah. John. Joel. |
| **talking about the retelling** | this talk, Wednesday, in Brunswick. |

His claim: wherever a human animates the room, there is energy — at **every**
rung. That is why a talk about a talk still works, and it is the strongest
evidence available, because rung three is the most degraded copy in the chain.

**Why energy beats the old framing.** The deck used to answer "why did that
work?" with an information argument: the hypothesis and the interpretation are
not in the data stream. True, and bloodless. Energy explains why a recording
fails **even when it contains every bit of the data** — because energy is not
in the data at all. It has to be made again, in a room, by a person, every time.

**Why the spoken word is "energy" and not "cognition."** Decision taken 13 Oct
on his authority. Energy is physical, needs no definition in a room, and the
organ makes it literal. Cognition requires the audience to accept a frame
before they can follow, which is one hurdle too many at minute nine in a
publick house. *Cognition stays out of the talk entirely.*

metaphor for the thesis; the volunteer is the thesis, in wood and leather,
unchanged since Roman antiquity.

### Item 4 — LOCKED. Hannah Bauer, the Donner Party. The catalogue, and the laugh.

**NNFC S2025E01, Thursday 30 January 2025, Wolverine Farm Publick House.**
*"Worst Party Ever: Everything You Didn't Know About the Donner Party."*

**What she did** — his account, 11 Oct: she presented a genuinely horrible
situation and made it funny by *momentarily putting the room into the mindset of
the people involved*, with some humorous perspectives.

**This is the strongest possible opening for item 4 and it should lead.** Not
because it is the funniest, but because it is the thesis performed live, on his
own stage, with a stopwatch on it:

- The Donner Party is the purest case of a chronicle with the people removed.
  The data is diaries, a body count, and a mountain pass. Complete, and inert.
- Hannah did not hand the room the data. She handed over the *point of view* —
  the hypothesis going in, to use the Mars framing — and **the room laughed about
  the Donner Party.** Nobody at Wolverine Farm was in the Sierra Nevada in 1846, and
  for a moment they were.
- Her own hook does his title flip for him: *"Most people hear 'Donner Party'
  and think 'cannibalism.' Valid — but that only scratches the surface."* Same
  move as the talk's own opening, made by somebody else, three years earlier.
- And her closing promise — *why it should be called something other than the
  Donner Party* — is a line that can be read aloud and left alone, the way the
  Berlioz title is.

**Running order inside item 4, if both go in:** Hannah first, Berlioz last. Her
hook is a setup and that title is a punchline, so they do not want to be adjacent
and they do not want to be in the other order.

**Two caveats, so neither becomes fact by repetition.**

1. **"He brought a pipe" was never a claim about Joel Bacon** — it was an
   illustration of the *form* a detail should take, written into the beat file
   and left sitting there looking like a memory. Moot now: the real Joel detail
   is far better and came from him. Struck so it cannot resurface.
2. **There is no `public/editions/S2025E01.json`.** The editions directory jumps
   from S2024E11 to S2025E06, so Hannah's Donner edition is not in the repo and
   the date above comes from his message and the Humanitix slug
   (`nerd-nite-fort-collins-s2025e01`), not from a record. Fine for the talk.
   Worth filling in after Melbourne, not before.

**All three talks are now in and each does different work.** Hannah is the
point of view, John is the editorial decision, Joel is the felt proof. Nothing
in item 4 needs a third entry — Berlioz is 30 seconds of title and that is all
the catalogue still wants.

**5) Name the recursion.** (0:45)
Out loud, deliberately, as the joke it is. You weren't at any of those. Often the
speaker wasn't at the thing either — Berlioz has been dead since 1869. So a talk
called "You Had to be There" has just spent four minutes on second-hand
material, and it worked. **Ask why, and do not answer yet.** That question is the
hinge of the whole talk and it buys the next two items outright.

**6) Mars.** (2:00)
We have not sent people. We have sent probes and rovers and instruments, and they
have returned more data than anyone can read. The telemetry is complete. The
chronicle is not. Someone had a hypothesis before that probe ever launched, and
someone else has a model in their head while they read what came back, and
neither of those is in the data stream.

This beat replaces the old item 6 ("the same content, flattened"), which was the
weak link and is now cut. Mars is better because it is vivid, everyone already
knows it, and **it never has to mention the machine** — probes are instruments,
full stop. The constraint holds for free.

**7) Where the cognition happens, and the debt paid.** (1:30)
The thesis, once, flat: it is where the people are that the cognition happens.

The abstract owes the room *"a few remarkable things I was in the room for,"* and
under this thesis those stop being biography and become the evidence. **The
strongest candidate is his own:** his first engineering job built the FEC and
digital physical layer for the NASA radio that carried voice between the Shuttle
and astronauts on spacewalks. An instrument whose entire job was carrying
*presence* — not telemetry, not findings, people talking to people. That pays the
abstract's debt and proves item 6 in the same breath, which no separate
biography beat can do.

The KC-130 is the optional second, and if it goes in it goes in as mechanism:
a fix was a crew act, not one man's. Past tense, never the opening. **One
example is enough. Two is the ceiling. His call.**

### Item 7 — NEW. "The abstract we didn't fix." John Anderson, Worm Majix.

**NNFC S2025E08, Thursday 18 September 2025, Wolverine Farm Publick House.**
*"Worm Majix"* — John Anderson. Shared the bill with Jenny Morse on poetry.

**What happened** — his account, 11 Oct. John is a long-time friend of Wolverine
Farm who used to watch the Nerd Nite crowd assemble. He approached Senne one
evening and was soon on the calendar. The abstract he sent was rough and hard to
follow. **Jamie started to edit it and he stopped her.** It ran exactly as
offered. John then gave a great talk — unfiltered John, dressed as he dresses for
any other evening at the Wolverine, speaking as himself.

His reason, verbatim: *"We want to platform people who want to talk. We want to
connect speakers and audience, not filter them."*

**This is the third kind of proof and the talk needs all three.** Hannah is the
*point of view*. The organ is the *felt* proof. This one is the *editorial*
proof, and it is the only beat where the thesis shows up as a decision somebody
had to make rather than an experience somebody had.

**Why it belongs between Mars and the organ.** Mars says the chronicle is
incomplete without the hypothesis of the builder and the model of the reader.
The obvious response to that is *so transmit better* — clean it up, smooth it,
make it portable. **This beat is the counter-example: the edit that would have
made the abstract clearer would have removed John from it.** The version that
read worse was the version that carried the person. He stopped the edit on
instinct and the talk proved him right.

*An interpretation, offered as one and not as a fact about him:* that is the same
mechanism as the HoloLens spec bug he carries from his own career — a legible
restatement silently becomes the reference, and clarity manufactures confidence
nobody earned. Same failure, opposite stakes, found at Wolverine Farm. He may want it and
he may not; it is one sentence either way and the beat does not need it.

**Strong recommendation: do not read John's abstract aloud.** It is the one move
that turns this beat cruel. Read out a rough paragraph to a room primed to laugh
and John becomes the joke, in front of an audience that includes other Nerd Nite
bosses. The beat does not need the exhibit. *It was rough. Jamie started fixing
it. I stopped her. We ran it as he sent it.* That is the whole thing, and the
roughness is the virtue rather than the punchline.

**What this beat is not:** a policy statement about curation. One line of reason,
past tense, then John on stage dressed exactly as he dresses, and move.

### Item 8 — RESTRUCTURED. Joel's organ is the landing, not one of the catalogue.

**NNFC S2026E06, Thursday 18 June 2026, Wolverine Farm Publick House.**
*"Let's Build a Pipe Organ!"* — Joel Bacon. From the listing: *"we'll assemble
hundreds of parts to form a fully functional, 2-octave, hand pumped, mechanical
action pipe organ."* (The mail blurb calls it "our May show." The edition record
says 18 June. The record is right.)

**What happened** — his account, 11 Oct: as Joel discussed the parts of the
instrument, his assistants and **the audience** assembled a working organ out of
them. Joel — professor, composer, professional musician — then brought it to
life. *"It connected me to the music in a way that I have never felt before. I
was personally moved when I heard a very talented musician work on the instrument
that we, as the audience, had just assembled."*

**This does not belong in item 4 and putting it there would waste it.** Item 4 is
the catalogue and the laugh. This is the only moment of genuine feeling in the
talk, and it is the answer to the question item 5 asks. It goes at the end.

**Why it is the keystone:** it is Mars, inverted, at Wolverine Farm in Fort Collins.

On Mars we send the instrument and the people stay home, and the chronicle comes
back incomplete. That night the people built the instrument **and stayed in the
room**, and what happened to him is not available in any recording of organ
music ever made. Same notes. Same physics. Identical data. The difference was
that he had helped put it together and was sitting there when it first spoke.

A recording of Joel playing that organ would contain every note and would not
contain the one thing that moved him.

**Consequences for the rest of the talk, all of them simplifications:**

1. **The abstract's debt is paid here, not by his career.** *"I have been lucky
   enough to be in the room for a few remarkable things. I will bring what I can
   of them."* The Donner talk and the organ night **are** remarkable things he
   was in the room for. The NASA voice radio drops to an optional one-sentence
   grace note and the KC-130 can come out entirely. Nothing in the talk now needs
   a career in it.
2. **It solves the hardest open item.** The standing note said the twenty seconds
   before the turn were the only lines that could not be scaffolded — the ones
   that carry the room from laughing to sitting still. This beat is that bridge,
   and the material is already his.
3. **Item 4 shrinks to 3:00** — Hannah at 2:30, Berlioz at 0:30. Worms left the catalogue entirely and became item 7 on its own merits.

**Revised budget:**

| 3) The flip | 1:30 |

| 5) Name the recursion. Ask why. Do not answer | 0:45 |

Forty-five seconds of margin. **First cut if it runs long: Berlioz, which is
30 seconds.** Second cut: trim the intro, which is the only item nobody will
miss. Never items 5, 7 or 8 — those three are the spine now.

**The two ways item 7 fails.**

*Explaining it.* "I have never been connected to music like that" said once,
flat, is the right amount. A second sentence about why is the sentimentality that
kills it. The pause does the work.

*Generalising it.* Not "and that's what Nerd Nite does." No instruction to the
room, not once, and that sentence would be the explanation that undoes the whole
beat eight seconds before the close.

**ANSWERED 11 Oct, and it is the best fact in the talk.** *"Yes, we needed a
volunteer to pump. Yes, that's right, that was part of what made it special."*

A hand-pumped organ cannot make a sound unless a person is physically standing
there supplying the air. **That instrument could not speak without a human being
in the room.** Not an analogy — the mechanism, by design, built in Roman
antiquity and still true at Wolverine Farm in June.

So the chain into the close writes itself and needs no gloss:
the organ needed somebody to breathe for it → *you had to be there* → *you are.*

**Say "a volunteer." Not "an audience member."** Somebody put their hand up.

**8) The turn, and the close.** (0:30)

**Budget:** items 4–7 are 8:15 against the 8:45 the body gets. Thirty seconds of

**Where it goes wrong:** item 5 answering its own question. If the "why" gets

minutes in the middle. Ask it and move on.

**What to drop if the laughs run hot in item 4:** the KC-130 out of item 7, then
the third talk out of item 4. Never Mars, and never item 5 — those two are now
the spine.

## Trajectory C — "You cannot plot a course without one" (not chosen)

The abstract already promised this one, nearly word for word: *a map between us,
an understanding of the world we are in. You cannot plot a course without one.*
This trajectory is the only one that collects on that line.

**4) The map.** (1:30)
Pick the promise back up from the abstract. A room like this draws a map between
the people in it. That is the claim; the next item is the mechanism.

**5) What you do when there is no map.** (2:30)
This is where the KC-130 belongs — mid-talk, as mechanism, never as a
credential, and never in the opening. Dead reckoning: known heading, known
speed, known time, and a correction every single time you get a real fix. You
fly a computed position and you do not trust it longer than you have to.
Then the hinge, in one line: **every talk you sit through is a fix.**

**6) The fixes.** (3:00)
Three talks off his stage. Same material as Trajectory B item 4, doing different
work — each one is a position report from somebody else's trajectory, handed over
by a person who was actually there.

**7) Trajectories converge.** (1:15)
Item 1's thought comes back and closes. Everyone in the room has been running
their own solution the whole time. Tonight is a common fix.

**8) The turn, and the close.** (0:30)

**Where it goes wrong:** the moment the metaphor gets explained it dies. It has
to be load-bearing and unmentioned — "every talk is a fix" said once, then never
referenced again. Also the only trajectory with the military material in a
structural position, which is the position the operating file says it must not
occupy as a credential. Mechanism is fine there; introduction is not.

**Strongest if:** he wants the talk to use his own vocabulary rather than Nerd
Nite's, and he is willing to let one metaphor carry three items without ever
naming it. **This is also the trajectory the Luke Delaney material fits**, if it
goes in at all — item 5, replacing the KC-130 rather than joining it. That call
is his and only his.

## Slides, given Tuesday

All three trajectories want the same slide deck, because the delivery is
extemporaneous and bullets would commit him to a script he does not want.

- **One slide per item, plus the talk titles.** Ten to twelve slides, not thirty.
- **One line of text, maximum, per slide.** A talk title set large is a slide. A
  photograph is a slide. A list of four sub-points is a teleprompter and it will
  flatten the delivery.
- **The Berlioz title gets a slide to itself** and he says nothing over it.
- Beamer, `\usetheme{default}`, no navigation bars, no frame numbers, no section
  headers. Dark ground, one typeface. The deck should be invisible.
- **No slide for the close.** The turn happens with nothing on the screen, or
  with whatever was already up. A slide that says "You are." does the work for
  him and robs it.
